Seasonal Food Guide

Eating seasonally means consuming foods that are naturally harvested during that time of year. Seasonal foods are often more flavorful, nutritious, and environmentally friendly. This guide helps you identify which foods are in season throughout the year.

Benefits of Eating Seasonally

  • Better Taste: Seasonal produce is harvested at peak ripeness, resulting in better flavor
  • Higher Nutrition: Fresh, in-season produce often contains more nutrients
  • Lower Cost: Seasonal foods are usually more abundant and less expensive
  • Environmental Benefits: Reduced transportation and storage needs mean lower carbon footprint
  • Supports Local Economy: Buying seasonal often means supporting local farmers

Spring Seasonal Foods (March - May)

Spring brings a variety of fresh, tender vegetables and fruits as the growing season begins.

Asparagus

Peak season in spring, asparagus is best when stems are firm and tips are closed.

Benefits: Rich in folate, vitamin K, and antioxidants. Supports digestive health.

Peas

Fresh peas are sweet and tender in spring. Look for firm, bright green pods.

Benefits: Good source of protein, fiber, and vitamins A, C, and K.

Spinach

Spring spinach is tender and flavorful. Choose leaves that are bright green and crisp.

Benefits: Packed with iron, calcium, and vitamins A and C. Supports eye health.

Strawberries

Early strawberries appear in late spring. Look for bright red berries with fresh green caps.

Benefits: High in vitamin C, manganese, and antioxidants. Supports heart health.

Radishes

Spring radishes are crisp and mildly spicy. Choose firm radishes with bright greens attached.

Benefits: Contains vitamin C, potassium, and antioxidants. Supports digestive health.

Artichokes

Spring artichokes are tender and flavorful. Look for tight, compact heads with green color.

Benefits: Rich in fiber, antioxidants, and prebiotics. Supports liver health.

Summer Seasonal Foods (June - August)

Summer brings an abundance of colorful fruits and vegetables at the peak of their flavor.

Tomatoes

Summer tomatoes are juicy and flavorful. Choose tomatoes that are firm but yield to slight pressure.

Benefits: Rich in lycopene, vitamins C and K. Supports heart health.

Corn

Fresh summer corn is sweet and tender. Look for bright green husks and moist silk.

Benefits: Contains fiber, B vitamins, and antioxidants. Supports eye health.

Watermelon

Summer watermelons are sweet and refreshing. Choose melons that feel heavy for their size.

Benefits: High in vitamins A and C, lycopene, and water content. Hydrating.

Zucchini

Summer zucchini is tender and versatile. Look for small to medium-sized zucchini with shiny skin.

Benefits: Low in calories, high in water and fiber. Contains vitamins A, C, and K.

Berries

Summer berries (blueberries, blackberries, raspberries) are sweet and juicy. Choose plump, firm berries.

Benefits: Rich in antioxidants, fiber, and vitamins. Support brain health.

Bell Peppers

Summer bell peppers are crisp and sweet. Look for firm peppers with bright, shiny skin.

Benefits: Excellent source of vitamins C and A. Support immune function.

Fall Seasonal Foods (September - November)

Fall brings hearty, comforting foods as the weather cools and harvest season peaks.

Apples

Fall apples are crisp and flavorful. Choose firm apples with no soft spots.

Benefits: Rich in fiber, vitamin C, and antioxidants. Support digestive health.

Pumpkins

Fall pumpkins are versatile and nutritious. Look for pumpkins that feel heavy for their size.

Benefits: High in vitamin A, fiber, and antioxidants. Support eye health.

Brussels Sprouts

Fall brussels sprouts are sweet and nutty. Choose firm, bright green sprouts.

Benefits: Rich in vitamins K and C, fiber, and antioxidants. Support bone health.

Sweet Potatoes

Fall sweet potatoes are sweet and nutritious. Look for firm sweet potatoes with no soft spots.

Benefits: Excellent source of vitamins A and C, potassium, and fiber. Support immune function.

Cranberries

Fall cranberries are tart and vibrant. Choose firm, plump berries with a deep red color.

Benefits: Rich in antioxidants and vitamin C. Support urinary tract health.

Kale

Fall kale is tender and flavorful. Choose kale with firm, deeply colored leaves.

Benefits: Packed with vitamins A, K, and C, calcium, and antioxidants. Supports bone health.

Winter Seasonal Foods (December - February)

Winter brings hearty root vegetables and citrus fruits that provide essential nutrients during the cold months.

Citrus Fruits

Winter citrus (oranges, grapefruits, mandarins) are juicy and vibrant. Choose fruits that feel heavy for their size.

Benefits: High in vitamin C and antioxidants. Support immune function.

Carrots

Winter carrots are sweet and crunchy. Look for firm carrots with vibrant color.

Benefits: Rich in beta-carotene, fiber, and vitamin K. Support eye health.

Cabbage

Winter cabbage is crisp and versatile. Choose firm, heavy heads with no discoloration.

Benefits: Contains vitamin C, fiber, and antioxidants. Supports digestive health.

Potatoes

Winter potatoes are hearty and satisfying. Look for firm potatoes with no green spots or sprouts.

Benefits: Good source of vitamin C, potassium, and fiber. Provide energy.

Beets

Winter beets are sweet and earthy. Choose firm beets with smooth skin and deep color.

Benefits: Rich in folate, manganese, and nitrates. Support heart health and exercise performance.

Winter Squash

Winter squash varieties (butternut, acorn, spaghetti) are sweet and nutritious. Look for squash that feels heavy for its size.

Benefits: High in vitamins A and C, fiber, and antioxidants. Support immune function.

Seasonal Eating Tips

  • Visit farmers' markets to find the freshest seasonal produce
  • Learn basic preservation methods (freezing, canning) to enjoy seasonal foods year-round
  • Be flexible with recipes and substitute with what's in season
  • Consider joining a CSA (Community Supported Agriculture) program
  • Grow your own herbs and vegetables when possible
