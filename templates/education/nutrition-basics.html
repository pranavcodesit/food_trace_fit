Nutrition Basics

Understanding the basics of nutrition is essential for making informed food choices. This guide covers the fundamental nutrients your body needs and why they're important.

Did You Know?

The food we eat provides the energy our bodies need to function. The amount of energy in food is measured in calories. An average adult needs about 2,000 calories per day, but this varies based on age, sex, weight, height, and activity level.

Essential Macronutrients

Macronutrients are nutrients that your body needs in large amounts. They provide energy and are essential for growth, metabolism, and other body functions.

Carbohydrates

The body's main source of energy. They are broken down into glucose, which cells use for energy. Complex carbs (whole grains, vegetables) are healthier than simple carbs (sugars).

Found in: Grains, fruits, vegetables, legumes

Proteins

Essential for building and repairing tissues, making enzymes and hormones, and supporting immune function. Made up of amino acids, some of which your body cannot produce (essential amino acids).

Found in: Meat, fish, eggs, dairy, legumes, nuts

Fats

Provide energy, help absorb vitamins, and protect organs. Unsaturated fats (olive oil, avocados) are healthier than saturated fats (butter, fatty meats) and trans fats (processed foods).

Found in: Oils, butter, nuts, seeds, fatty fish, avocados

Essential Micronutrients

Micronutrients are nutrients that your body needs in smaller amounts. They don't provide energy but are crucial for various bodily functions.

Vitamins

Organic compounds needed for normal cell function, growth, and development. There are 13 essential vitamins, each with specific roles.

Examples: Vitamin A (vision), Vitamin C (immune function), Vitamin D (bone health)

Minerals

Inorganic elements that help with various bodily functions like building bones, regulating heartbeat, and carrying oxygen.

Examples: Calcium (bones), Iron (blood), Potassium (heart function)

Water

Often overlooked but crucial for life. It helps regulate body temperature, transport nutrients, remove waste, and lubricate joints.

Recommendation: About 8 cups (2 liters) daily, more with exercise or hot weather

Balanced Diet Principles

A balanced diet includes a variety of foods that provide all the nutrients your body needs:

  • Variety: Eat different types of foods from all food groups
  • Moderation: Don't eat too much or too little of any food or food group
  • Adequacy: Eat enough to meet your nutritional needs
  • Balance: Don't let any one food group dominate your diet

Nutrition Tip

The more colorful your plate, the more likely you are to get a good variety of nutrients. Different colored fruits and vegetables contain different beneficial compounds.
